# Free ski tracking on iPhone in 2026: what you actually get

A ski tracker can be free to download and still keep its best
numbers behind a subscription. The real question is what's free to
*use*. Slopes is a clear example of
the free-plus-subscription model: its
[own pricing page](https://getslopes.com/premium) lists unlimited
recording, a daily stats summary, resort conditions and trail maps
as free, while run-by-run stats, virtual 3D mountains, speed
heatmaps, offline trail maps, level-of-effort insights and
side-by-side run comparison are in Premium, at $34.99 a year
("price may vary in different countries").

That model is legitimate (running servers costs money), but it means
the free tier records your day while the per-run analysis is paid.

## Is there a ski tracker for iPhone that's completely free?

Yes — that's Alpy's bet. No premium tier exists, because the app has
no servers to fund: maps come from
[OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), weather from
[Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/), AI runs on the device, and
your data stays on the phone. So the whole instrument panel is free:

- Per-run metrics: time, drop, top/average speed, estimated turns,
  course, and accelerometer airtime estimates when it catches a jump
- Personal records on every named run, with a race chart against
  your own best
- Live ghost racing while you ski a run you've skied before
- Day replay on 3D terrain; 3,652 named OpenStreetMap ski areas to
  pick from, with their OSM trail maps where volunteers have mapped them
- Powder alerts, freeze curve, official avalanche bulletins

The things we *don't* have are the things that need a server:
friends on the map across a mountain (Slopes lists that under
Premium), cloud sync, social feeds. If those matter to you, a
subscription app is honestly the right tool — our
[comparison with Slopes](/compare/) says so in a table.

"Free" on a store page can mean "free sample." It can also just mean
free — when the architecture makes the costs disappear rather than
the business model hiding them.
