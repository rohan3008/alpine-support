# Free ski tracking on iPhone in 2026: what you actually get

Search "ski tracker" and every top result is free to download. The
real question is what's free to *use*. The pattern across the
category in 2026: basic recording is free; the analysis layer is
subscription. Per-run performance stats, interactive resort maps,
3D replays, fitness insights — in the market leaders, those live
behind "Premium" at roughly the price of a day-ticket per year.

That model is legitimate (servers and licensed maps cost money), but
it means the free tier is mostly a diary, with the interesting
numbers held back.

## What "actually free" looks like

Alpy's bet is different: no premium tier exists, because the app has
no servers to fund — maps come from OpenStreetMap, weather from
Open-Meteo, AI runs on the device, and your data stays on the phone.
So the whole instrument panel is free:

- Per-run metrics: time, drop, top/average speed, estimated turns,
  course, airtime estimates
- Personal records on every named run, with a race chart against
  your own best
- Live ghost racing while you ski a run you've skied before
- Day replay on 3D terrain, trail maps for 3,652 OSM ski areas
- Powder alerts, freeze curve, official avalanche bulletins
- Apple Watch heart-rate readout per run (read-only, from Health)

The things we *don't* have are the things servers buy: friend
tracking across a mountain, cloud sync, social feeds, licensed resort
artwork. If those matter to you, a subscription app is honestly the
right tool — our [comparison with Slopes](/alpine-support/compare)
says so in a table.

"Free" in app stores usually means "free sample." Occasionally it can
just mean free — when the architecture makes the costs disappear
rather than the business model hiding them.
