# The 2026/27 season-opening checklist

First chair of the season is coming. Here's a checklist that earns its place —
organized by body, gear, paperwork, safety and phone, with the
reasoning attached, because a checklist you understand is one you
actually run.

## How to get ready for ski season, step by step

Start about six weeks before first chair: leg work and a binding
check first, then pass math, insurance and avalanche-kit practice two
weeks out, then charge, cache and set alerts the night before. The
timings are our suggestion; the one hard deadline is pass pricing,
which can step up through autumn (details below).

1. **Six weeks out: start the leg work.** Slow eccentric squats,
   step-downs, wall-sits and balance work, two or three short
   sessions a week.
2. **Six weeks out: book the binding check.** Release tested against
   your DIN, edges, bases and wax while you're there; try your boots
   on.
3. **Two weeks out: do the pass math and sort insurance.** Realistic
   days times honest day-ticket prices, and cover that explicitly
   includes piste rescue.
4. **Two weeks out: rebuild the bulletin habit and run a transceiver
   drill.** Fresh batteries, one genuine practice search.
5. **The night before: charge, cache, set thresholds.** Phone at
   100%, trail map cached, powder alert set.
6. **First morning: track the baseline day** — and ski like it's the
   first day, because the data will cheerfully confirm that it is.

The reasoning behind each step follows.

## What training should you do before ski season?

Train the eccentric contraction skiing actually uses — muscles
lengthening under load. A
[study of recreational skiers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5117052/)
notes that eccentric knee-extensor (quadriceps) activity is dominant
during turns, and its 24 middle-aged intermediate skiers still had
reduced eccentric hamstring strength (and, in one leg, quadriceps
strength) 24 hours after a four-hour session. The authors suggest
eccentric training "may be warranted" for recreational skiers.

Our suggested routine is simple: slow downhill-phase squats and
step-downs (take three to four seconds on the way down), reverse
lunges, wall-sits, and single-leg balance work — eyes closed once it
gets easy. Two or three short sessions a week, with rest days
between.

## Do ski bindings need checking every season?

We'd say yes — it's the one item we treat as non-negotiable:
**bindings checked by a shop, every season**, so the release setting
is confirmed before you need it.

The release setting (the "DIN" number) is chosen from your weight,
height, age, skier type and boot sole length — the parameters listed
in the free preview of the
[ISO 11088 shop standard](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/71932/a1cc6d2778bc4e65afc9610f902a78d5/ISO-11088-2018.pdf) —
so tell the shop if any of those changed. While you're there: edges
deburred, bases ground if last spring's rocks left their signature,
and a fresh wax.

Boots: check liners for packed-out heel pockets and buckle teeth for
wear. Try boots on an evening *before* the trip, not in a rental
queue at altitude.

Helmet: Smith says
[most manufacturers recommend replacing within 3–5 years](https://smithopticsaustralia.com/blogs/news/when-to-replace-your-ski-helmet-a-guide-from-smith),
and immediately after a significant fall or collision, because the
internal foam can be compressed even when the shell looks intact.
If you can't remember buying it, that's your answer.

## Paperwork: the pass math has a deadline

Some multi-resort passes price-step upward through autumn — as we
write,
[epicpass.com](https://www.epicpass.com/) carries a "Prices Go Up
November 15" banner — so the arithmetic is worth twenty minutes now
rather than December: count your realistic days, multiply by honest
day-ticket prices at your target resorts, compare. The 2026/27
wrinkle worth knowing: on our [resort pages](/resorts/), checked
against the official pass sites in October 2026, big Alpine names on
Ikon such as Zermatt, Chamonix and Kitzbühel are *limited-days*
(7 days on the Ikon Pass, 5 on the Base Pass, per
[Ikon's destinations page](https://www.ikonpass.com/en/destinations)) —
fine for a trip, not a season. And if you ski in Europe: carrier or
travel insurance that explicitly covers piste rescue and
repatriation.

## Safety: the bulletin habit and the beeping drill

Two pre-season items for anyone who leaves the groomers. First,
rebuild the **bulletin habit** before the season builds its
snowpack: find your region's official avalanche bulletin
([SLF](https://www.slf.ch/en/) for Switzerland,
[avalanche.org](https://avalanche.org/) for the US) and start reading it weekly
from the first storms — persistent weak layers
[can last weeks to months](https://www.avalanches.org/standards/avalanche-problems/),
and following their life story beats meeting them by surprise. Our [bulletin-reading primer](/blog/reading-avalanche-bulletins/)
covers the five-step scale, aspects and elevations.

Second, if you carry rescue kit: **batteries and a practice run**.
Fresh batteries in the transceiver, and one genuine practice search
— garden, park, anywhere — because the first rehearsal of the
season shouldn't be the real one.

## Phone: five minutes before first chair

The sensor-lab in your pocket needs a little setup to earn its
keep on day one:

- **Charge discipline:** Apple designs iPhone for 0–35 °C ambient
  and says very cold conditions outside that range
  [can temporarily shorten battery life](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118431).
  Start at 100% and carry it in an inside pocket.
- **Permissions while calm:** a tracking app will ask for location
  and motion access on first start — grant them at breakfast, not
  with gloves on in a gondola.
- **Offline fallbacks:** download or cache your resort's trail map
  before leaving wifi. In Alpy, catalog resorts' piste maps and
  local guides ship inside the app; a piste map fetched live is kept
  on the phone for 30 days, and the last conditions it fetched show
  (with their timestamp) when there are no bars.
- **Powder alerts on now:** set your fresh-snow threshold before
  the season's first storm, not after reading about it.
- **A baseline day:** your first day back is worth tracking even
  though you'll ski it slowly — it's the season's baseline. Every
  PR and every fatigue trend the rest of the winter is measured
  against somewhere, and day one is where the record book opens.
