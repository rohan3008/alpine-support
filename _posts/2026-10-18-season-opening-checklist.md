# The 2026/27 season-opening checklist

First chair of the season is six-odd weeks out for most of the
Alps and North America. Here's a checklist that earns its place —
organized by body, gear, paperwork, safety and phone, with the
reasoning attached, because a checklist you understand is one you
actually run.

## Body: train the contraction you'll actually use

Skiing punishes quadriceps *eccentrically* — muscles lengthening
under load as they absorb terrain — and eccentric strength is
specific: cycling and running help your lungs but transfer poorly
to the braking strength that keeps you off the backseat at 3pm.
Six weeks is enough to move the needle with embarrassingly simple
work: slow downhill-phase squats and step-downs (take three to four
seconds on the way down), reverse lunges, and wall-sits for the
static endurance that traverses and crud demand. Add single-leg
balance work — eyes closed once it gets easy — because edge
recovery is a balance reflex, not a strength feat. Two or three
short sessions a week beats one heroic one; eccentric training
generates famous soreness, and the adaptation needs rest days to
land.

## Gear: the binding check is not optional

The one genuinely non-negotiable item: **bindings tested, every
season**. Release settings drift, springs age, and a binding that
won't release is a knee-ligament lottery ticket. Shops test release
torque against your DIN setting in minutes — and your DIN is a
function of weight, boot sole length, age and ability, so tell the
shop if any of those changed. While you're there: edges detuned of
rust burrs, bases stone-ground if last spring's rocks left their
signature, and a fresh wax (summer storage dries bases out).

Boots: check liners for packed-out heel pockets (heel lift is where
shin bang and blisters start) and buckle teeth for wear. Try boots
on an evening *before* the trip — feet change, and discovering it
in a rental queue at altitude is the expensive way.

Helmet: five-ish years or one real impact, whichever comes first —
EPS foam is single-use by design. If you can't remember buying it,
that's your answer.

## Paperwork: the pass math has a deadline

Multi-resort passes (Epic, Ikon, Mountain Collective, Magic Pass)
price-step upward through autumn and some close sales entirely, so
the arithmetic is worth twenty minutes now rather than December:
count your realistic days, multiply by honest day-ticket prices at
your target resorts, compare. The 2026/27 wrinkles worth knowing:
big-name Alpine resorts on Ikon are mostly *limited-days* (5–7),
fine for a trip, not a season; Magic Pass remains absurd value if
you ski Switzerland often. We keep verified per-resort coverage on
our [resort pages](/resorts/), checked against the official pass
sites in October 2026. And if you ski in Europe: carrier or travel
insurance that explicitly covers piste rescue and repatriation —
a helicopter off an Alpine piste is a four-figure event uninsured.

## Safety: the bulletin habit and the beeping drill

Two pre-season items for anyone who leaves the groomers. First,
rebuild the **bulletin habit** before the season builds its
snowpack: find your region's official avalanche bulletin (SLF for
Switzerland, avalanche.org for the US) and start reading it weekly
from the first storms — early-season weak layers are the ones that
haunt February, and following their life story beats meeting them
by surprise. Our [bulletin-reading primer](/blog/reading-avalanche-bulletins/)
covers the five-step scale, aspects and elevations.

Second, if you carry rescue kit: **batteries and a practice run**.
Fresh batteries in the transceiver, and one genuine practice search
— garden, park, anywhere — because transceiver skills decay over a
summer and the first rehearsal shouldn't be the real one. Kit you
can't use at speed is kit you're carrying for morale.

## Phone: five minutes before first chair

The sensor-lab in your pocket needs a little setup to earn its
keep on day one:

- **Charge discipline:** cold murders lithium batteries; an inside
  pocket near your body can be 20% of range at -15 °C. Start at
  100%.
- **Permissions while calm:** a tracking app will ask for location
  and motion access on first start — grant them at breakfast, not
  with gloves on in a gondola.
- **Offline fallbacks:** download or cache your resort's trail map
  before leaving wifi; Alpy caches piste networks, conditions and
  guides automatically after first view, precisely for the no-bars
  bowl on the back side.
- **Powder alerts on now:** set your fresh-snow threshold before
  the season's first storm, not after reading about it.
- **A baseline day:** your first day back is worth tracking even
  though you'll ski it slowly — it's the season's baseline. Every
  PR and every fatigue trend the rest of the winter is measured
  against somewhere, and day one is where the record book opens.

## The honest summary

Six weeks out: start the leg work and book the binding check. Two
weeks out: pass math, insurance, bulletin habit, transceiver drill.
The night before: charge, cache, thresholds. First morning: track
the baseline day and ski like it's the first day — because the
data will cheerfully confirm that it is.
