# The 3,652 ski areas OpenStreetMap knows about

Commercial ski apps advertise big resort counts — Slopes, for one,
[announced "over 2,000+ resorts"](https://news.getslopes.com/slopes-app-expands-to-2-000-resorts-worldwide-on-interactive-3d-maps/)
on interactive maps for Premium subscribers in November 2024. The
number sounds like a moat. But by that same announcement, 1,523 of
those maps come from OpenStreetMap.

There's a parallel universe where that work already happened in
public. OpenStreetMap contributors map ski areas —
[pistes with names and difficulties, lifts with their
stations, area boundaries](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Pistes).

## How many ski areas are in OpenStreetMap?

By our count, 3,652 named ones. In October 2026 we indexed every
*named* [winter-sports area](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:landuse%3Dwinter_sports)
in OSM: **3,652 areas worldwide**, from Zermatt to Norwegian
local ski lifts like Fidjeland Skitrekk, each with coordinates and terrain
elevation. Unnamed areas aren't in that number, and the count will
move as mappers add more.

## How good is OpenStreetMap ski data?

It varies, because it's community-mapped: OSM is
[built by a community of mappers](https://www.openstreetmap.org/about),
and coverage follows where they've worked. An OSM area isn't
"supported" in the verified sense; it's mapped, and quality
varies — if a resort near you is thin, anyone who maps in OSM can
fix it. Alpy labels these entries exactly that way, next to a
smaller curated catalog of 51 resorts whose coordinates we checked
against live geocoding and elevations against a terrain model.

## What it makes possible

Search any of the 3,652 areas in Alpy and you get whatever trail
network OSM has mapped there, drawn two ways — a clean schematic
chart and an interactive 3D view — plus live weather for its
coordinates and full GPS tracking. For free, because the mapping was
a commons to begin with. The app credits OpenStreetMap
([ODbL](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)) on screen. Catalog
resorts' piste maps ship inside the app; any other area's map is
fetched from the public
[Overpass](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API) servers
(the main instance is operated by FOSSGIS, and its usage policy
says "cache and rate-limit calls") and kept for 30 days.

The subscription model is legitimate — hand-built maps take real
work. But "thousands of resorts" isn't much of a moat when, in
Slopes' own count, most of them come from a commons anyone
can use.
