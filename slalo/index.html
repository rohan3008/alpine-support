Slalo: Ski Lessons

Learn from beginner to powder in 3D, on iPhone. Get it on the App Store

Slalo teaches skiing with an animated 3D skier, from your first snowplough to powder and trees. Each lesson breaks one technique into a few steps. Tap a step and the skier shows it: the parts of the body that matter light up, and labels on the body point at what to watch.

The lessons

Eleven lessons in four sections, laid out down one trail on the home screen.

Beginner

  • Snowplough glide (green run): make a wedge, keep your speed down and press one ski to turn.
  • Stopping (green): push your heels out and press the inside edges until you come to a stop.
  • First parallel turns (blue): start each turn with a small wedge, then bring the skis together.

Intermediate

  • Carving (blue): tip the skis onto their edges so they grip and follow a clean arc.
  • Short turns (red): quick, rhythmic turns where the legs turn and the body keeps facing down the hill.

Advanced

  • Moguls (black): your legs fold over each bump and stretch into each dip.
  • Ice and hardpack (red): get the edges biting early and keep your upper body quiet so the outside ski holds.
  • Ready for blacks (30° slope): commit forward and down the hill when your instinct says lean back.
  • Jump turns (38° slope): plant the pole, hop, and swing both skis across the hill in the air.

Off-piste

  • Powder: stay centred, weight both skis evenly and bounce from turn to turn.
  • Trees (glade): look at the gaps and keep your turns short and in control.

Each lesson has its steps, a "Why it works" page and a list of common mistakes with a fix for each. You can switch the skier to the common mistake to compare, turn on the Balance or Angles lens, change the camera or drag to orbit, freeze the turn at Transition, Edging, Apex or Release, and add a second camera view. Each step starts at half speed so you can see the move, then speeds up to show the flow, and you can set the speed yourself. The Carving lesson has a calculator for edge angle and turn radius from your speed and your ski's sidecut.

Ticks, quiz and ski pins

A step gets a tick once you've watched it for a few seconds. When every step in a lesson is ticked, a short quiz unlocks: in each of three rounds the skier makes one turn and you say whether it was good or the mistake. Get two of the three right and the lesson is complete. You can retry the quiz at any time.

Each finished lesson puts an enamel-style ski pin on your pin board in the app. There is a pin for each section too, and a Whole mountain pin when all 11 lessons are done. A bar on the home screen shows how many lessons you've finished.

Questions

Does it cost anything?

Slalo is a one-off purchase on the App Store. There are no ads, no in-app purchases and no account.

Is it for beginners?

Yes. The first three lessons cover the snowplough, stopping and your first parallel turns. Use them alongside lessons with an instructor, and start on a nursery slope with flat ground at the bottom.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. Everything is in the app, so it works offline, including in airplane mode.

What data does it collect?

None. Slalo has no analytics and makes no network requests. See the privacy policy.

Where is my progress stored?

Only on this iPhone. Your ticks, quiz results and pins are saved on the phone, and Slalo never sends them anywhere. To start again, open About from the home screen and choose Reset progress. Deleting the app also deletes your progress.

Is the skier filmed or motion-captured?

No. The motion is generated by code from the physics of each turn, then simplified so the technique is easy to see. The angles and speeds on screen come from that model. They show the idea; they aren't measurements of a real skier.

How accurate is the carving calculator?

It uses simplified physics: sin(edge angle) ≈ speed² ÷ (9.81 × sidecut radius), and turn radius ≈ sidecut radius × cos(edge angle). Treat the numbers as a guide.

Can it replace a ski instructor?

No. Slalo explains technique so you know what to work on. Learn new terrain with an instructor.

Are powder and tree skiing safe?

Both are off-piste, where avalanches are possible. Never go alone, check the avalanche forecast, and carry a transceiver, shovel and probe you know how to use. In trees, stay clear of tree wells: the deep, loose hollows that can form round trunks.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone with iOS 26 or later, in portrait.
