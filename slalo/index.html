Slalo: Ski Technique Lessons

Carving, moguls & trees in 3D. Free for iPhone.

Slalo teaches ski technique with an animated 3D skier. Each lesson breaks one skill into a few steps. Tap a step and the skier shows it: the parts of the body that matter light up, and labels pinned to the body point at what to watch.

The lessons

  • Carving (blue run): turn on the edges of your skis instead of skidding them round.
  • Short turns (red): quick, rhythmic turns where the legs turn and the body keeps facing down the hill.
  • Moguls (black): your legs fold over each bump and stretch into each dip.
  • Trees (off-piste glade): look at the gaps, keep your turns short and in control.
  • Ready for blacks (30° slope): commit forward and down the hill when your instinct says lean back.

Each lesson has its steps, a "Why it works" page and a list of common mistakes with a fix for each. You can switch the skier to the common mistake to compare, turn on the Balance or Angles lens, change the camera or drag to orbit, slow it to ¼ or ½ speed, freeze the turn at Transition, Edging, Apex or Release, and add a second camera view. The Carving lesson has a calculator for edge angle and turn radius from your speed and your ski's sidecut.

Questions

Does it cost anything?

No. Slalo is free, with no ads, no in-app purchases and no account.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. Everything is in the app, so it works offline, including in airplane mode.

What data does it collect?

None. Slalo has no analytics and makes no network requests. See the privacy policy.

Is the skier filmed or motion-captured?

No. The motion is generated by code from the physics of each turn, then simplified so the technique is easy to see. The angles and speeds on screen come from that model. They show the idea; they aren't measurements of a real skier.

How accurate is the carving calculator?

It uses simplified physics: sin(edge angle) ≈ speed² ÷ (9.81 × sidecut radius), and turn radius ≈ sidecut radius × cos(edge angle). Treat the numbers as a guide.

Can it replace a ski instructor?

No. Slalo explains technique so you know what to work on. Learn new terrain with an instructor.

Is tree skiing safe?

Tree skiing is off-piste. Never go alone, check the avalanche forecast, and stay clear of tree wells: the deep, loose hollows that can form round trunks.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone with iOS 26 or later, in portrait.
